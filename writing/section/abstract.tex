\begin{abstract}
\mname enhances low-light images in a polar Horizontal/Vertical/Intensity (\hvi) color space
whose intensity-dependent chroma collapse is claimed to remove color noise near black, and it
is trained with a four-term loss duplicated in sRGB and \hvi. Its published \dataset numbers
come from single runs selected on the test split: the paper reports \phm{28.2}~dB with
GT-mean rescaling, while two third-party retrainings report \phm{23.6} and \phm{24.0}~dB. We ask under which conditions, and by how much, each design step
reproduces. Inside one frozen \mname we build two nested single-factor ladders: five loss arms
(full recipe, no VGG term on \hvi, $k$ detached, RGB only, $\Ck$-weighted chroma) and five
representation arms (learned $k$, fixed $k$, no collapse, linear chroma, luma intensity), each
trained from scratch with five matched seeds, selected on a scene-disjoint validation split,
read on the test split once at the final checkpoint, and judged by paired-by-seed tests fixed
before launch. The seed SD of GT-mean PSNR, the primary metric, is \phm{0.16}~dB, so the
minimum detectable step is \ph{0.5}~dB.
\hvi-space supervision contributes \ph{0.6}~dB over RGB-only training; the intensity collapse
contributes \ph{0.4}~dB, located in the three darkest intensity deciles as its mechanism
predicts; the VGG term on \hvi, the loss-side coupling of $k$, learning $k$, and the
max-versus-luma intensity choice are each removable or inconclusive at $\pm 0.3$~dB. Test-split checkpoint selection inflates raw PSNR by \phm{0.41}~dB but GT-mean PSNR by only
\phm{0.06}~dB; the published spread is mostly GT-mean rescaling (\phm{3.39}~dB) and the
test-time saturation gate (\phm{1.09}~dB), and every absolute number we report is under
our protocol.
\end{abstract}
