\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Low-light image enhancement recovers a well-exposed image from an underexposed capture,
and learned enhancers now dominate the paired benchmarks such as \dataset
\citep{wei2018retinex, cai2023retinexformer, guo2020zerodce}. A recent line of work argues
that the color space in which the network operates and is supervised matters as much as
the architecture: \mname \citep{yan2025hvi} maps images into a polar Horizontal/Vertical/
Intensity (\hvi) space, enhances intensity and chroma along separate branches, and is
trained with a loss summed over both the RGB output and its \hvi transform.

The cost of that recipe is that it bundles several design choices together. The training
objective has eight terms (L1, SSIM, edge, and VGG perceptual, each applied in RGB and in
\hvi), the network width is fixed, and the input is fed raw. The original paper reports the
combined system; it does not report which of these choices carries the benefit, nor how
much of the reported gap to other methods survives seed-to-seed variation.

Existing ablations of enhancement networks typically vary one factor at a time on a single
seed, and select the reported checkpoint on the test set. Both practices make small
differences look decisive.

\begin{center}
\emph{Which components of the dual-space \mname recipe \\ measurably improve enhancement
quality, \\ and which are indistinguishable from seed noise?}
\end{center}

We answer this with a controlled study rather than a new method. Every run in this paper
shares one frozen protocol: the same \dataset train/validation split, the same optimizer
and learning-rate schedule, FP32 training on \hw, checkpoint selection by validation PSNR
only, and a held-out test split that no training or selection code ever reads. The
protocol is hash-pinned, and each result number in this paper is read from the run's
result file by a verification script rather than typed.

Our key observation is that the exploratory seed is not representative. An intensity-only
input prefilter improved LPIPS and SSIM on seed 42, but on two fresh seeds it fails a
pre-registered screen (\Cref{sec:experiments}). We therefore report every comparison with
its seed spread, and treat a difference smaller than that spread as no difference.

Concretely, we contribute: (1) a leave-one-out ablation of all eight loss terms of \mname
at two widths, which shows that the \hvi-space perceptual term accounts for \ph{0.3}\,dB
of validation PSNR while the remaining \hvi-space terms are within \ph{0.1}\,dB of noise;
(2) a width sweep from 18 to 36 base channels under the same protocol, which gains
\ph{0.6}\,dB PSNR for a \ph{4}$\times$ parameter increase; and (3) a pre-registered
confirmation study of input prefiltering, which does not replicate the exploratory gain.
