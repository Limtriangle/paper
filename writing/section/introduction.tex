\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Learned low-light image enhancement recovers a well-exposed image from an underexposed
capture, and the paired \dataset benchmark \citep{wei2018retinex} has been the field's main
yardstick from Retinex decomposition networks \citep{wei2018retinex, zhang2019kind} through
normalizing flows \citep{wang2022llflow} and Retinex transformers
\citep{cai2023retinexformer} to diffusion models \citep{hou2023gsad}. A recent line of work
argues that the color space in which the network operates and is supervised matters as much
as the backbone \citep{guo2023bread, brateanu2025lytnet, guan2025cstnet}. \mname
\citep{yan2025hvi} is the most visible instance: it maps sRGB into a polar
Horizontal/Vertical/Intensity (\hvi) space in which hue is an angle, saturation a radius, and
the radius is shrunk toward the origin as intensity approaches zero by a learned collapse
function $\Ck$; two interacting branches enhance intensity and chroma, and the network is
trained with a loss of four terms (L1, SSIM, edge, VGG) applied once in sRGB and once in
\hvi. It reports the strongest \dataset numbers among the methods it compares, and follow-ups
adopt or modify the space \citep{shi2025fusionnet, yang2026rhvifdd, ai2026bcihv,
cheng2026vcr, wang2026haimnet}.

The evidence behind the design is thinner than its adoption suggests. Every number in the
paper and in its follow-ups is a single training run; no work in this family reports a seed
spread. The released code selects checkpoints on the \dataset test split, which has fifteen
images, and ships several checkpoints per dataset named by the metric they maximize; we
measure that this inflates raw PSNR by \phm{0.41}~dB but GT-mean PSNR by only
\phm{0.06}~dB. The headline \dataset value, \phm{28.2}~dB \citep{yan2025hvi}, is reported after
rescaling the output to the ground-truth mean and with a test-time saturation gate; third-party retrainings report \phm{24.0}~dB (GT-mean status not stated)
\citep{li2026pal} and \phm{23.6}~dB (presumably without it) \citep{hvicidnet_issue66}; the authors' own
\phm{23.50}~dB \citep{shi2025fusionnet} equals their released weights without GT-mean
\citep{hvicidnet_readme}. The color-space ablation used a proxy network, and the loss weights
($1$, $\phm{0.5}$, $\phm{50}$, $\phm{0.01}$, in two spaces) are stated nowhere. A reader cannot tell which parts of the design carry the result.

Follow-ups do not settle this. They replace the intensity definition \citep{yang2026rhvifdd,
ai2026bcihv}, the branch interaction \citep{wang2026haimnet, wu2026tcanet}, or the loss
\citep{li2026pal}, and each reports a single-run gain over the previous single run. The one
matched comparison of \hvi against LAB and YCbCr that exists puts YCbCr ahead on LOL-v2
\citep{shi2025tpcnet}, and the authors' own later ablation on \dataset ranks the spaces
differently \citep{yan2025hvicidnetplus}. A three-seed SD of \phm{0.2}--\phm{0.4}~dB, reported for motion deblurring
\citep{shafi2026iphoneblur}, would account for most of these margins. We therefore ask:

\begin{center}
\emph{Under a frozen protocol with matched seeds, which steps of \mname's representation \\
and loss change \dataset quality by more than the seed spread, by how much, and where?}
\end{center}

Our angle is to measure rather than to propose. Swapping a whole color space changes four
things at once: the branch split, the space of the auxiliary loss, the learnable $k$, and the
space of the residual connection. We instead build two \emph{nested single-factor ladders}
inside one \mname, so that neighbouring arms differ in exactly one step, and we evaluate each
step with a paired-by-seed test whose decision rules, equivalence margin, and metric were
fixed before the first run. The loss ladder walks from the released recipe to RGB-only
supervision in four steps; the representation ladder walks from learned-$k$ \hvi to YCbCr in
four steps while the loss stays frozen; every arm shares architecture, parameter count,
schedule, validation split, and the single final-checkpoint test read.

The key observation that makes the study decidable is that the published spread is not one
number. Under our protocol, rescaling the output to the ground-truth mean adds \phm{3.39}~dB to a
raw \phm{23.25}~dB and the saturation gate adds \phm{1.09}~dB more; picking the best of
\phm{101} checkpoints on the test split adds only \phm{0.06}~dB under GT-mean; the seed SD of
GT-mean PSNR is \phm{0.16}~dB. Once those are separated, a step of \phm{0.34}~dB is detectable with five seeds on the
loss ladder.

We make four contributions. (1)~A frozen, fully specified protocol for \mname with a
scene-disjoint validation split, matched seeds, final-checkpoint read-out, and one test read
per arm (\Cref{sec:method}). (2)~A loss ladder: \hvi-space supervision contributes
\phm{0.58}~dB over RGB-only training, the VGG term on \hvi tensors and the coupling of $k$ through the loss are removable at
$\pm 0.3$~dB, and a $\Ck$-weighted chroma loss is inconclusive (\Cref{sec:experiments}).
(3)~A representation ladder under the frozen loss: the intensity collapse $\Ck$ contributes
\ph{0.4}~dB, and its gain sits in the three darkest intensity deciles as its mechanism
predicts, while learning $k$, polar versus linear chroma, and max versus luma intensity are
each removable or inconclusive. (4)~A decomposition of the published \dataset spread into rescaling, gating, selection,
and seed noise, with the seed SD and the minimum detectable effect stated for every claim.
