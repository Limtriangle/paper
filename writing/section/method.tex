\section{Two Ladders inside One Network}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Model.} We use the public \mname implementation \citep{yan2025hvi} unchanged:
an sRGB image is mapped to a three-channel working space, an intensity branch takes the
intensity channel and a chroma branch takes all three channels, twelve cross-attention
blocks (six per branch, one of which in the intensity branch receives no gradient because
the released code overwrites its output before use) couple the two, the network predicts a residual in the working space, and the result is
mapped back to sRGB. In \hvi the working space is $I = \max(R,G,B)$ and
$(H, V) = \Ck(I)\, S\, (\cos h, \sin h)$ with $\Ck(I) = (\sin(\pi I/2) + \epsilon)^{k}$, where
$k$ is a learned scalar initialised at \phm{0.2} and $S, h$ are HSV saturation and hue. Every arm
below keeps the branch split, the residual in its own working space, the block structure,
and the parameter count (\phm{1975569}, asserted per run); only the step named in its row
changes.

\paragraph{Loss ladder (S1).} The released objective is
$\mathcal{L} = \ell(\hat{x}, x) + \lambda\, \ell(T_k(\hat{x}), T_k(x))$ with
$\ell = \mathrm{L1} + \phm{0.5}\,\mathrm{SSIM} + \phm{50}\,\mathrm{edge} + \phm{0.01}\,\mathrm{VGG}$, $\lambda = 1$,
and $T_k$ the forward \hvi transform through which the learned $k$ also receives gradient.
Two properties of this recipe are unexamined: the VGG term is applied to \hvi tensors after a
range shift that leaves the sRGB input in $[0.5, 1]$, and $k$ can lower the second term by
collapsing chroma in both prediction and target. The ladder removes one property per step on
the \hvi representation: L0 is the released recipe; L1 drops the VGG term from the \hvi
loss; L2 additionally detaches $k$ in the loss transform, fixing it at the released converged
value $k_0 = 1.1255$; L3 sets $\lambda = 0$ (RGB only); L4 returns to L2 and weights the \hvi
chroma terms by $\Ck(I_{\mathrm{gt}})$, so that pixels whose chroma the transform has
collapsed count in proportion. L2 is the frozen loss of the second ladder.

\paragraph{Representation ladder (S2).} Under L2, five arms change one aspect of the working
space each: A0 is \hvi with learned $k$; A1 fixes $k$ at the median converged value of the A0
runs; A2 sets $k = 0$, which removes the collapse and leaves Cartesian HSV
$(I, S\cos h, S\sin h)$; A3 keeps $I = \max(R,G,B)$ but replaces polar chroma by the linear
opponent channels $(C_b, C_r)$; A4 is YCbCr, replacing the max intensity by luma. Each
transform is invertible on the valid range and differentiable, and the round-trip error of
every adapter is recorded in the preflight file of the run. The nested contrasts
A0$-$A1, A0$-$A2, A2$-$A3, A3$-$A4 isolate learning $k$, the collapse, the chroma
parametrisation, and the intensity definition. A1 is dropped only if $k$ moves by less than
$0.05$ in the A0 runs.

\paragraph{Frozen protocol.} All 45 runs share one protocol, pinned by the SHA-256 of the
training script, the upstream commit, and the split file in every run manifest. Training
follows the released code: 1000 epochs, batch 8, $256^2$ crops, random flips, Adam at
$10^{-4}$ with a 3-epoch warm-up and cosine decay to $10^{-7}$, on \hw. The 485
\dataset training pairs are split into \phm{445} training and 40 validation pairs by scene, using
DINOv2 and perceptual-hash clusters so that no validation scene appears in training and no
validation cluster contains a test image. Each arm is trained with the five seeds
$\{42, \dots, 46\}$, which fix data order, crops, flips, and initialisation of the
shape-identical layers, so that the seed is a blocking factor across arms. Validation is
scored every five epochs; the \emph{primary read-out is the final checkpoint}, the
best-validation checkpoint is secondary, and the test split is read once per run by a
separate script at the end. A post-hoc oracle scores every tenth checkpoint on the test
split for \Cref{sec:selection} only and never selects anything. Test-time gates and gains
are off in every arm.

\paragraph{Metrics.} The primary metric is PSNR after one scalar luminance gain that matches
the prediction's mean to the ground truth (GT-mean), because the released code, the paper,
and most baselines report \dataset that way and because a raw-PSNR difference that vanishes
under the gain is a brightness bias, not an enhancement difference. We report raw PSNR, SSIM,
LPIPS, CIEDE2000 before and after the gain, and the log-exposure error beside it, and we
decompose raw MSE into a gain term and a residual. For the mechanism claim we compute, per
arm and seed, RGB error, CIEDE2000, and chroma-weighted circular hue error in ten deciles of
ground-truth intensity, plus a darkness stress test in which the test input is scaled by
$0.5$ and $0.25$ with fixed Poisson--Gaussian noise.

\paragraph{Decision rules.} For each contrast we take the five paired-by-seed differences of
the primary metric and test them with a paired $t$-test, Holm-adjusted across the four
contrasts of its ladder. A step \emph{contributes} when the adjusted CI excludes zero, the
mean difference is at least $0.3$~dB and more than twice its SD, the sign agrees in at least
four seeds, and a mixed model with image and seed intercepts agrees in direction. A step is
\emph{removable} when two one-sided tests at $\pm 0.3$~dB reject equivalence's complement at
$\alpha = 0.05$. Otherwise it is \emph{inconclusive}, and we report the minimum detectable
effect. The mechanism claim for $\Ck$ holds only if the A0$-$A2 hue and CIEDE2000
differences have CIs excluding zero in the three darkest deciles while the five brightest
deciles show none or a difference at least three times smaller, and the stress-test
difference grows with darkening. These rules, the loss weights, $k_0$, the split, and the
analysis code were frozen with hashes before the first ladder run.

\paragraph{What the ladders do not test.} The architecture and the two-branch split are
fixed, so an sRGB arm without a luma/chroma split is not on the ladder (the released
ablation's sRGB row is a different architecture), and a different backbone might prefer a
different space. Training uses one dataset, and LOL-v2-Real cannot test transfer
(\Cref{sec:app-cross}). Polar HSV
with a wrapped hue axis is omitted because the paper already shows it fails; A2 is HSV
without the wrap. An inconclusive step is not shown to be useless, only smaller than the
\phm{0.34}~dB that five seeds resolve here.
