\section{Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

Each ladder is reported as its four nested contrasts (\Cref{tab:s1,tab:s2}): the paired
difference in the primary metric between neighbouring arms, its seed SD, the Holm-adjusted
interval, and the verdict in the vocabulary of \Cref{sec:method}.

\paragraph{Reproduction under the frozen protocol.} The five L0 runs of the released
recipe reach a raw test PSNR of \ph{23.8}~dB and \ph{27.7}~dB after the GT-mean gain, with a
seed SD of \ph{0.3}~dB on the validation split. The raw value lies inside the window
\ph{23.3}--\ph{24.3}~dB spanned by the authors' released checkpoint and independent
retrainings, so the protocol reproduces the published model, and the seed SD sets the paired
minimum detectable effect at \ph{0.5}~dB for every contrast below. The window was fixed
before the runs as the pipeline gate.

\paragraph{Loss ladder.} \Cref{tab:s1} reports the four nested contrasts. Removing the VGG
term from the \hvi-space loss changes GT-mean PSNR by \ph{0.1}~dB, within the equivalence
margin: the perceptual term on non-RGB tensors is removable. Detaching $k$ from the loss
transform changes quality by \ph{0.0}~dB and is inconclusive on quality alone, but it
changes what $k$ does: with the coupling, $k$ drifts from $0.2$ to \ph{1.1} over training,
and without it $k$ moves only through the residual path. Removing \hvi-space supervision
entirely costs \ph{0.6}~dB, so the second loss space contributes, and this is the one step of
the released recipe that survives the seeded test. Weighting the \hvi chroma terms by the
collapse $\Ck(I_{\mathrm{gt}})$ is equivalent to the uniform weighting at $\pm 0.3$~dB. The
frozen loss used in the representation ladder, L2, therefore differs from the released
recipe by two removable steps.

\paragraph{What the coupling of $k$ does.} In the released code the inverse transform uses
$k$ as a detached constant, so $k$ receives gradient only through the forward transform:
through the \hvi-space loss on both the prediction and the target, and through the residual
connection. Coupling $k$ into the loss therefore lets the network shrink the second loss
term by collapsing chroma in the target as much as in the prediction, which is the shortcut
that L2 removes. That the quality difference between L1 and L2 is inconclusive while the
trajectory of $k$ changes shows that the learned collapse is a property of the objective as
much as of the representation, and it is why the representation ladder is run under the
detached loss rather than the released one.

\paragraph{Representation ladder.} \Cref{tab:s2} isolates the four representation steps
under L2. Because $k$ moves from $0.2$ to \ph{1.1} in the A0 runs, far above the $0.05$
threshold at which A1 would have been dropped, the learned-versus-fixed contrast is real,
and it is removable: fixing $k$ at the converged value changes nothing the seeds can
resolve. Removing the
collapse altogether (A0$-$A2) costs \ph{0.4}~dB and is the one representation step that
contributes; the per-decile analysis places its gain in the three darkest intensity deciles,
where the A0$-$A2 hue-error and CIEDE2000 differences exclude zero while the five brightest
deciles show none, and the difference grows monotonically under the darkness stress test.
This is the pattern the collapse mechanism predicts. Replacing polar chroma by linear
opponent chroma at the same intensity (A2$-$A3) changes quality by \ph{0.1}~dB and is
inconclusive, and replacing the max intensity by luma (A3$-$A4) changes raw PSNR by
\ph{0.2}~dB but GT-mean PSNR by \ph{0.0}~dB: the max-versus-luma choice is an exposure
effect, absorbed by one scalar gain, and the raw-MSE decomposition places the whole raw
difference in the gain term. The representation ladder thus ends where the loss ladder
did: one step carries the design, and it is the one the mechanism story is about.

\paragraph{Seed variance and selection bias.}
\label{sec:selection}
Across arms the seed SD of test GT-mean PSNR is \ph{0.3}~dB. The post-hoc oracle, which
scores every tenth checkpoint on the test split, exceeds the final checkpoint by
\ph{0.3}~dB on average, and its maximum over seeds, the number a paper would report after
test-split selection, exceeds the mean final checkpoint by \ph{0.6}~dB. Together with the
\ph{3.9}~dB added by the GT-mean gain, these three quantities account for the
\ph{23.5}--\ph{28.2}~dB spread of published \dataset values for the same model. None of the
oracle numbers is used to rank arms; they exist to size the practice, and they are why the
primary read-out is the final checkpoint: a selected one imports this bias into every contrast.

\paragraph{Transfer to LOL-v2-Real.} Scoring every final checkpoint on the LOL-v2-Real test
split, deduplicated against the \dataset training set and without retraining, preserves the
two verdicts that contribute: \hvi-space supervision and the collapse keep their sign in
every seed, and the removable steps stay within $\pm 0.3$~dB. The absolute level drops for
every arm by the same amount to within the seed SD, as expected for a model trained on
\dataset alone, and we make no claim about the ranking of arms on the second dataset beyond
the sign agreement; a verdict that flipped there would be reported as a flip.

\paragraph{The recipe that follows.} Under this protocol, a practitioner keeps the \hvi
working space with its collapse and a second loss space, and may drop the VGG term on \hvi
and the loss-side coupling of $k$ at no measurable cost; the choice between learned and fixed
$k$, between polar and linear chroma, and between max and luma intensity is a matter of
convenience at the \ph{0.5}~dB resolution of five seeds. Full per-arm tables, per-decile
analysis, cross-dataset scores, and the oracle decomposition of the brightness-shift failure
are in \Cref{sec:app-experiments}.
