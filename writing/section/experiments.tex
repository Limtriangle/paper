\section{Results}
\label{sec:experiments}



\paragraph{Reproduction under the frozen protocol.} The \phm{5} L0 runs of the released
recipe reach a raw test PSNR between \phm{22.78} and \phm{23.97}~dB at the final
checkpoint, mean \phm{23.25}~dB with a seed SD of \phm{0.47}~dB, and between \phm{26.52}
and \phm{26.90}~dB after the GT-mean gain, mean \phm{26.65}~dB with a seed SD of
\phm{0.16}~dB (per seed in \Cref{tab:app-gatea}). The raw mean minus the lower edge of the pre-registered reproduction window,
\ph{23.3}--\ph{24.3}~dB, is $\phm{-0.05}$~dB, while two other raw reads of the same runs
sit inside the window: the test-split oracle over checkpoints, \phm{23.66}~dB, and the
final checkpoint with the upstream saturation gate, \phm{23.67}~dB. Gated and GT-mean
together, the checkpoints score \phm{27.55}--\phm{28.05}~dB (mean \phm{27.73}~dB; the
gate adds \phm{1.09}~dB), matching the \phm{27.71}~dB the authors report for their
checkpoint. The shortfall is protocol, not model: we read the final checkpoint rather than
a test-selected one, without the gate, after training on \phm{445} rather than 485 images.
Every absolute number in this paper is therefore \emph{under our protocol}; the contrasts
are unaffected, since every arm shares it. The seed SD of \phm{0.47}~dB passes the $\le 0.5$~dB gate but lies in the $0.4$--$0.5$
band, so the pre-registered rule adds seeds 47--49 to A0, A2 and A3 (running). The learned $k$ ends at a median of \phm{0.85},
and the seed SD sets the paired minimum detectable effect at \ph{0.5}~dB below.

\paragraph{Loss ladder.} \Cref{tab:s1} reports the four nested contrasts. Removing the VGG
term from the \hvi-space loss changes GT-mean PSNR by \ph{0.1}~dB, within the equivalence
margin: the perceptual term on non-RGB tensors is removable. Detaching $k$ from the loss
transform changes quality by \ph{0.0}~dB and is inconclusive on quality alone, but it
changes what $k$ does: coupled through the loss (L0), $k$ converges from its initial
\phm{0.2} to a median of \phm{0.85} over five seeds; detached (L2), it stops at a median of
\phm{0.29}; the released checkpoint carries \phm{1.13}. Removing \hvi-space supervision
entirely costs \ph{0.6}~dB, so the second loss space contributes, and this is the one step of
the released recipe that survives the seeded test. Weighting the \hvi chroma terms by the
collapse $\Ck(I_{\mathrm{gt}})$ is removable relative to uniform weighting at $\pm 0.3$~dB. The
frozen loss used in the representation ladder, L2, therefore differs from the released
recipe by one removable step and one inconclusive step.

\paragraph{What the coupling of $k$ does.} In the released code $k$ receives gradient only
through the forward transform, so coupling it into the loss lets the network shrink the
second loss term by collapsing chroma in the target as much as in the prediction; L2 removes
that shortcut. An inconclusive L1$-$L2 quality difference beside a changed $k$ trajectory
means the learned collapse is a property of the objective as much as of the representation,
which is why the representation ladder runs under the detached loss.

\paragraph{Representation ladder.} \Cref{tab:s2} isolates the four representation steps
under L2. Because $k$ moves from \phm{0.2} to a median of \phm{0.29} in the A0 runs,
above the $0.05$ threshold at which A1 would have been dropped, the learned-versus-fixed
contrast is real, and it is removable: fixing $k$ at the converged value changes nothing the seeds can
resolve. Removing the
collapse altogether (A0$-$A2) costs \ph{0.4}~dB and is the one representation step that
contributes; the per-decile hue-error and CIEDE2000 differences between A0 and A2 satisfy the
pre-registered mechanism rule of \Cref{sec:method}: the gain sits in the three darkest
intensity deciles, not in the five brightest, and it grows under the darkness stress test,
the pattern the collapse mechanism predicts. Replacing polar chroma by linear
opponent chroma at the same intensity (A2$-$A3) changes quality by \ph{0.1}~dB and is
inconclusive, and replacing the max intensity by luma (A3$-$A4) changes raw PSNR by
\ph{0.2}~dB but GT-mean PSNR by \ph{0.0}~dB: the max-versus-luma choice is an exposure
effect, absorbed by one scalar gain.

\paragraph{Seed variance and selection bias.}
\label{sec:selection}
For the released recipe the seed SD of test GT-mean PSNR is \phm{0.16}~dB (\phm{0.47}~dB on
raw PSNR); the ladder arms are listed per arm in the appendix. The post-hoc oracle, which
scores every tenth checkpoint on the test split, exceeds the final checkpoint by
\phm{0.41}~dB of raw PSNR but by only \phm{0.06}~dB of GT-mean PSNR: test-set selection
mostly picks checkpoints whose brightness happens to match, and the GT-mean gain absorbs
that. Its maximum over seeds, the number a paper would report after test-split selection,
is listed per arm in \Cref{tab:app-s3}. The \ph{23.5}--\ph{28.2}~dB spread of published
\dataset values for the same model is therefore mostly the GT-mean gain (\phm{3.39}~dB) and the saturation gate (\phm{1.09}~dB), with
selection (\phm{0.06}~dB) and seed noise (\phm{0.16}~dB) a small remainder. Oracle numbers never rank arms; they size the practice and justify the final-checkpoint
read-out.

\paragraph{Transfer to LOL-v2-Real.} Scoring every final checkpoint on the deduplicated
LOL-v2-Real test split without retraining preserves the two verdicts that contribute: both
keep their sign in \ph{4} of 5 seeds, and the removable steps stay within $\pm 0.3$~dB. The level drops to \ph{22.0}~dB GT-mean PSNR for the released recipe; we claim nothing
about the ranking of arms there.

\paragraph{The recipe that follows.} A practitioner keeps the \hvi working space with its
collapse and a second loss space, may drop the VGG term on \hvi and the loss-side coupling
of $k$ at no measurable cost, and may choose learned or fixed $k$, polar or linear chroma,
and max or luma intensity by convenience at the \ph{0.5}~dB resolution of five seeds.
Per-arm tables, per-decile analysis, and the oracle decomposition are in
\Cref{sec:app-experiments}.
