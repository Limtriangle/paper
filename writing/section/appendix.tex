\subsection{Protocol Details}
\label{sec:app-protocol}

Each run directory records the training-script SHA-256, the upstream \mname commit, the
split-file SHA-256, the torch version, and the GPU name. The validation summary written at
the end of every run and the single test read by the final-evaluation script are the only
sources of the numbers in this paper; every table is generated from those files and every
number in the text is bound to the key it was read from. Training runs in the default
non-deterministic CUDA mode; two runs with the same seed differ by \phm{0.04}~dB on
validation, which we report as the run-to-run floor beneath the seed SD. The upstream
gradient clipping, which the released code calls before the backward pass and which
therefore does not act, is kept unchanged so that L0 is the released recipe. The 24 training
images that share a scene with a test image stay in the training set, as in every published
\dataset result; we state this because 14 of the 15 test images have a near-duplicate in
training, which bounds how far any \dataset number generalises.

\subsection{Mechanism Analysis}
\label{sec:app-mechanism}

For the A0$-$A2 contrast we report, per seed, the difference in chroma-weighted circular hue
error and in CIEDE2000 in each decile of ground-truth intensity, with seed-level 95\%
confidence intervals, and the same difference under the darkness stress test at input
scales $1$, $0.5$, and $0.25$ with frozen Poisson--Gaussian noise. The pre-registered rule
requires the bottom three deciles to exclude zero, the top five to include it or to be at
least three times smaller, and the stress-test difference to grow with darkening. The
per-decile curves for every other contrast are reported descriptively, without a rule.

\subsection{Cross-Dataset Scores}
\label{sec:app-cross}

The plan scored every final checkpoint on the LOL-v2-Real test split \citep{yang2021lolv2}
as a cross-dataset check. That check is not possible for models trained on \dataset: of its
\phm{100} test pairs, \phm{91} have a ground truth pixel-identical to a \dataset training
ground truth (the same \phm{91} are flagged by perceptual hash and by DINOv2 embedding),
and \phm{0} match a \dataset test image. Only \phm{9} images from \phm{3} scenes remain.
LOL-v2-Real's test split therefore cannot serve as a generalisation test for \dataset-trained
models, and no cross-dataset claim is made in this paper. Because at least five images
survive, each loss-arm final checkpoint is scored once on the \phm{9} remaining images as a
descriptive read; with \phm{3} scenes these reads carry no verdict and are not used by any
contrast. Mean GT-mean PSNR over seeds on the \phm{9} images is \phm{30.15}~dB for L0,
\phm{29.77}~dB for L1, \phm{29.98}~dB for L2, \phm{28.74}~dB for L3, and \phm{30.50}~dB
for L4; every arm has \phm{5} seeds except L1 with \phm{4}, whose seed-44 read failed twice
and is not repeated. These numbers are listed, not ranked.

\subsection{Mixed-Model Check for the Loss Ladder}
\label{sec:app-mixed}

For L2$-$L3 the pre-registered mixed model agrees in direction (coefficient \phm{0.58}).
Its seed variance sat at the zero boundary (\phm{0.005}) and the fit did not converge, so
we also fit an image-only random-intercept model as a robustness check outside the rules,
which converges and gives the same estimate (\phm{0.58}). The frozen rule tests the sign
of the mixed-model coefficient, not its convergence flag, so the verdict does not depend on
this check.


\subsection{Oracle Decomposition of the Brightness-Shift Failure}
\label{sec:app-s4}

\mname degrades sharply when the input brightness moves away from the training distribution
\citep{pilligua2025mill}. This chapter asks how much of that loss is one scalar of exposure
miscalibration, using no training. On the validation split we blend each input toward its
ground truth at $\alpha \in \{0.1, 0.2, 0.35, 0.5\}$ and scale it by $\{0.5, 2, 4\}$, then
compare three per-image oracles that read the ground truth: identity, a closed-form
mean-matching pre-gain, and a joint search over the pre-gain and \mname's inference knobs
$(k, \alpha_s, \alpha_i, \gamma)$. Scenes are the replication unit (\phm{18} scenes, scene-bootstrap 95\% intervals).
A single pre-gain recovers a fraction \phm{0.755} of the loss that the full oracle recovers
on shifted inputs. The full oracle adds \phm{2.48}~dB raw PSNR beyond the gain
[\phm{2.04}, \phm{2.98}] and \phm{3.15}~dB GT-mean PSNR [\phm{2.69}, \phm{3.62}], so the
pre-registered condition that the knobs add less than $0.3$~dB beyond a gain fails.
The split of that extra recovery is descriptive: nearly the same gap, \phm{2.31}~dB
[\phm{1.65}, \phm{3.01}], appears on unshifted inputs, so it is not specific to the shift;
its chroma and hue part is $\phm{-0.10}$ CIEDE2000 units [$\phm{-0.35}$, \phm{0.16}], so it
is not a color correction; and the selected $k$ stays at a median of \phm{1.125}, the
released value. The extra recovery is per-image oracle flexibility on tone. The validation
images are part of the released model's training data, so these reads describe a model on
images it has memorised. Because every oracle selects its knobs with the ground truth, the
numbers are upper bounds on what adaptation could recover, not a method.
