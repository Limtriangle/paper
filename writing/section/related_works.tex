\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Retinex-based enhancement.} Retinex theory \citep{land1977retinex} models an
image as the product of illumination and reflectance, and most learned low-light enhancers
descend from it. RetinexNet \citep{wei2018retinex} learns the decomposition end to end and
introduced the \dataset benchmark; KinD \citep{zhang2019kind} enhances illumination and
reflectance along separate branches; URetinex-Net \citep{wu2022uretinex} and RUAS
\citep{liu2021ruas} unroll the decomposition into a network, the latter with architecture
search. Retinexformer \citep{cai2023retinexformer} folds the illumination estimate into a
one-stage transformer and is the published model closest in size to \mname.

\paragraph{Supervised enhancers in other formulations.} LLFlow \citep{wang2022llflow} models
the conditional distribution of normal-light images with a normalizing flow; SNR-Aware
\citep{xu2022snr} routes between local and global processing by a spatial signal-to-noise
estimate; LLFormer \citep{wang2023llformer} targets ultra-high-definition inputs; GSAD
\citep{hou2023gsad} and Diff-Retinex \citep{yi2023diffretinex} use diffusion models. The
channel-attention block inside \mname follows the design of Restormer
\citep{zamir2022restormer}. LEDNet \citep{zhou2022lolblur} treats enhancement and deblurring
jointly and introduced the LOL-Blur dataset.

\paragraph{Unsupervised and zero-reference methods.} Zero-DCE \citep{guo2020zerodce}
estimates per-pixel enhancement curves without paired data; EnlightenGAN
\citep{jiang2021enlightengan} trains adversarially on unpaired images; SCI \citep{ma2022sci}
learns a self-calibrated illumination map with a very small network. \mname is trained with
pairs only and its authors list unsupervised training as open.

\paragraph{Color spaces for enhancement.} Most of the methods above operate on sRGB, and the
HSV space that \mname starts from dates to \citet{smith1978hsv}. Bread \citep{guo2023bread}
splits an image into luminance and chrominance and lets the enhanced luminance guide a
chrominance mapper; LYT-Net \citep{brateanu2025lytnet} processes Y and UV along separate
lightweight paths; DCC-Net \citep{zhang2022dccnet} separates a gray image from an explicit
color histogram; Ucolor \citep{li2021ucolor} embeds RGB, HSV and Lab features jointly; CoLIE
\citep{chobola2024colie} fits an implicit illumination field in HSV; CSTNet
\citep{guan2025cstnet} learns a YCbCr-like transform for nighttime deraining, and CATformer
\citep{wu2026catformer} learns a von Kries adaptation before CIELAB. \mname
\citep{yan2025hvi} instead maps sRGB into a polar Horizontal/Vertical/Intensity (\hvi)
space in which hue is an angle, saturation a radius, and the radius is shrunk toward the
origin as intensity goes to zero by a learned collapse function; the network enhances
intensity and chroma along two interacting branches and is supervised in both sRGB and
\hvi. An earlier version of the method \citep{yan2024onecolorspace} and a successor for
extreme darkness \citep{yan2025hvicidnetplus} exist; we study the CVPR 2025 version whose
code is public.

\paragraph{Work that builds on or questions \hvi.} FusionNet \citep{shi2025fusionnet}
fuses \mname with two sRGB enhancers and won the NTIRE 2025 challenge
\citep{liu2025ntire}; DiffLight \citep{feng2024difflight} and Q-CIDNet
\citep{verma2025qcidnet} reuse the network for diffusion-based and video enhancement, and
JarvisIR \citep{lin2025jarvisir} calls it as an off-the-shelf tool. Several works modify the
intensity or the fusion: RHVI-FDD \citep{yang2026rhvifdd} argues that the max-RGB intensity
is sensitive to noise spikes, BC-IHV \citep{ai2026bcihv} replaces the linear intensity by a
learnable Box-Cox law, VCR \citep{cheng2026vcr} recalibrates channels by their variance,
HAIMNet \citep{wang2026haimnet} and TCA-Net \citep{wu2026tcanet} change how the two
branches interact, and TPCNet \citep{shi2025tpcnet} adds physical constraints. Others report
sensitivity of \mname to input brightness \citep{pilligua2025mill, du2026atp} or to
per-pair photometric mismatch in the training data \citep{li2026pal}. None of these reports
a seed spread, and none compares color spaces under one fixed network with more than one run.

\paragraph{Evaluation practice.} Model selection on the test split is a documented source
of inflated benchmark numbers \citep{musgrave2020reality, recht2019imagenet}, and
single-run comparisons hide variance that changes conclusions
\citep{bouthillier2021variance, pineau2021reproducibility}. For low-light enhancement in
particular, \citet{nguyen2024diffusiondark} document scene overlap between the \dataset
train and test splits, and \citet{liao2025gtmean} show that the brightness mismatch hidden
by GT-mean rescaling is systematic. We therefore select checkpoints on a scene-disjoint
validation split, read the test split once, report every mean with its seed spread, and
report the GT-mean and gated variants separately from the primary numbers.

\paragraph{Datasets and metrics.} We use the paired \dataset benchmark of
\citet{wei2018retinex}; its successor LOL-v2 \citep{yang2021lolv2}, SID \citep{chen2018sid},
SICE \citep{cai2018sice}, and LOL-Blur \citep{zhou2022lolblur} are the other paired sets on
which \mname was reported. Full-reference quality is measured by PSNR, SSIM
\citep{wang2004ssim}, and LPIPS \citep{zhang2018lpips}; no-reference quality by NIQE
\citep{mittal2013niqe} and BRISQUE \citep{mittal2012brisque}. The perceptual loss term of \mname is the VGG feature loss of
\citet{johnson2016perceptual}. Color fidelity, when we report it, uses the CIEDE2000
difference \citep{sharma2005ciede2000}.
